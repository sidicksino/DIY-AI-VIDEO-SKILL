# Growth playbook — research 2026-11-15

What the breakout videos in our niche actually do, where we differ, and the
rules every new Luna video follows. Re-run this research every ~2 months.

## What was studied

- **Our channel:** YouTube Studio screenshots (28 days to 2026-11-13) and
  our 10 public Shorts. Instagram and TikTok are not connected to vidIQ, so
  there are no numbers for them yet.
- **10 competitor channels:** their last 21–30 Shorts each (286 uploads):
  dates, views, lengths.
- **27 breakout videos** (0.4M–131M views): 23 measured frame by frame
  (cuts, length, the frames at 0 / 0.5 / 1 / 1.5 / 2 / 3 s, the last frame),
  4 of the very biggest watched in full for story and sound.
- **30 Instagram and TikTok breakouts** (0.5M–21M views): vidIQ's hook,
  format and sound notes.

Evidence files: `ideas/research-2026-11-15/` (opening-frame sheets,
`analysis.json`, channel lists).

## What the breakouts do

### 1. The first second is already moving (27 of 27)

| Opening | Count | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Mid-action on the junk or the empty space** in the first 0.5 s | 16 | teddy bear pushed into a cement mixer · sawing a tyre · pouring flowers on a bare floor · spraying an outline on a cliff |
| **A person or animal in a bad place, or a small conflict** | 9 | neighbour splashes her book · woman mocked in a dumpster · old couple floating in a green pool · old man asleep in a ruined room |
| A single cover frame (1 frame), then straight into one of the two above | 5 of the 27 | finished room or before/after card |
| **A 1+ second flash of the finished piece first (what we did)** | **0** | — |

Nobody shows the result first. The result is the reward for watching, and
the opening asks the question: *what is she making from that?*

### 2. Something changes every 1–2 seconds

- **Montage style** (13 videos): average shot 0.9–2.0 s. Abigail's 131M
  video has 22 cuts in 48 s. Dream Structures' 1.16M video has 10 cuts in
  15 s.
- **Fixed-camera time-lapse** (11 videos): one wide camera that never
  moves, and something new appears every second (tyres stack up, walls go
  up, furniture pops in).
- Ours: average shot 2.3–3.7 s, with single shots of 6–9 s (018 has 9.1 s
  and 7.3 s; 017 has 6.0 s and 6.4 s).

### 3. A reason, shown without words

All four of the biggest (131M, 23.7M, 15.1M, 1.2M) set up a reason in the
first 2–3 s and close it at the end:

- splashed by the neighbour → builds her own pool → splashes him back
- mocked in a wrecked boat → builds a beach cabin → the mockers clap
- old couple in a filthy pool → pool cleaned → they cannonball in
- white lace dress + power saw on a tyre → swing lounger → sips tea on it

### 4. The ending is someone enjoying it, and it is short

Sitting on it, jumping in, sipping tea, people reacting. The final beauty
shot is 2–3 s. They do not loop seamlessly; the before/after contrast is
what makes people rewatch.

### 5. Sound: loud, crisp real sounds. No music in 3 of the 4 biggest

A distinct sound on every beat (saw, drill, thud, tape snap, pour, splash).
No voice-over. **No on-screen text in 4 of 4.** Our "no music, no text"
rules match the biggest hits. On Instagram most breakouts use a music beat;
on TikTok about half use raw sound with effects.

### 6. Titles tell the story

- "He Splashed Her Book. She Built a Pool." (131M)
- "The Stairs Gave Up. She Didn't." (2.1M)
- "The Weeds Moved Out. The Lounge Chairs Moved In." (1.6M)
- "These Empty Bottles Got a Glowing Retirement" (1.2M)

Two short sentences: the problem, then the answer. No hashtags in the title.

## The rules (every new video)

**R1. Open in action.** Frame 0 shows Luna already doing something to the
junk or the empty place, or the problem beat. Never open on the finished
piece. The finished piece appears only in the last third.

**R2. Make step one the striking action.** The story stays in order, so
choose the build so that its first step is the surprising one (sawing the
tyre, tipping a bucket of bottle caps on the bare floor, dipping the towel
in concrete). Luna may walk in carrying the junk, but movement is visible
within 0.5 s and the first tool-on-junk contact has started by 1.0 s.

**R3. Give a reason.** One beat of 1.5–3 s that shows who or what it is
for: a problem, a need, a small funny conflict. Shown, never written. It
is closed at the end.

**R4. A change every 1–2 seconds.** No shot longer than 2.5 s, unless it
is a fixed-camera time-lapse in which something new appears every second.
Use 2–3 short trims from one clip instead of one long trim.

**R5. Show more steps.** Quick build: 8–12 beats. Big transformation:
20–30 beats.

**R6. End on use, keep it short.** The last 3–4 s: the finished piece in
its place, and someone using it or reacting (Luna, a guest, a child, a
pet). The smooth camera travel stays, but 3.5 s at most.

**R7. Two formats.**

| Format | Length | What | How often |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Quick build** | 15–22 s | one object, 8–12 beats | daily |
| **Big transformation** | 45–60 s | a whole room, yard, terrace or wreck, with a reason, 20–30 beats | 2 a week |

**R8. Sound on every beat.** Each clip prompt names one clear, loud sound
for its action. No music, no voice, no text.

**R9. Titles.** Alternate the "I Turned X Into Y" pattern with a
two-sentence story title. Hashtags go in the description, not the title.

**R10. Measure and repeat.** Every week, note each video's "viewed vs
swiped away" and average percentage viewed in `ideas/log.md`. Make more of
the opening type and format that wins.

## Seen but not adopted

- **On-screen text and cover cards:** some channels use them. The four
  biggest don't. Our rule stays: none.
- **Music:** common on Instagram. We keep Flow's own sound. Worth one test
  later on Instagram only, if the user wants.
- **People in distress, rats, bullying:** it works, but keep Luna's world
  warm. Use light problems: a pet with no bed, a guest with nowhere to sit,
  a dull corner, a neighbour's nicer garden.
- **A contrasting outfit** (white lace dress with a power saw): strong
  hook, but Luna keeps her own look.
